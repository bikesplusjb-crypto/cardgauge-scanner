01Scanning a batch

Seven steps, start to finish. The order matters — backs have to be captured before you scan, not after.

1

Lay the cards out

Three to ten at a time, flat, with a gap between each one. No overlapping.

Why: the app finds a card by seeing all four of its edges. Cards that touch each other get cropped together and priced as one.
2

Stand back and shoot

Hold the phone high enough that there's clear table visible all the way around the group. Even light, no direct glare.

Why: further away reads better than closer, which is the opposite of what feels right. A card pressed to the edge of the frame is the most common cause of a bad crop.
3

Type how many cards you put down

Use the small count box before pressing Detect.

Why: this is the only thing that catches a card being missed. Without it, finding four of five looks exactly like success.
4

Detect cards

Check the count that comes back matches what you put down. If it's short, re-shoot before going any further.

Why: everything after this runs on the crops. A bad crop can't be fixed later in the process.
5

Capture backs

Flip every card in place without sliding them around, then take the second photo from the same distance.

Why: the year and card number are printed on the back. Skip this and cards come back as "Unknown" year with a price for the player rather than the card.
6

Scan all detected

Wait for every tile to finish. Each one comes back with a name, a price, and sometimes a warning.

Why: prices come from completed eBay sales over the last 30 days, not asking prices — unless the tile says otherwise.
7

Add all to inventory

Read the flagged cards first, then press once and wait for the confirmation.

Why: nothing is saved before this. Pressing twice adds everything twice.
Before step 7, nothing exists. Scan results live in the page only. Reloading, closing the tab, or switching to another device loses them. Add to inventory is the moment it becomes real and shows up for your whole team.

02Reading the badges

Every tile tells you where its number came from. That's the difference between a price you can put on a sticker and one you should check.

Sold

A median of what this card actually sold for. This is the number to trust.

Ask only

No completed sales found, so this is what sellers are asking. Asking prices run high — treat it as a ceiling, not a value.

Mixed comps

Sold data exists but the sales don't agree with each other. Usually means parallels got mixed in. Worth a look.

Needs review

Something didn't add up — the set isn't in the catalog, or the listings disagree with the year on the card. Check before you price it.

Crop looks too small

The app grabbed a piece of a card rather than the card. Re-detect that one.

Front + back matched

Both sides were used to identify this card. These reads are the most reliable.

03Pricing so you actually make money

Set your real numbers once in Business Profile and every screen below uses them.

FieldWhat to putWhat it changes
Target margin20%The margin you want to keep on a card
Marketplace fee13%eBay's cut — comes off every sale
Shipping cost$1.00What it costs you to post one card

The Margin & Discount Guide then shows four things per card. Margin after fees is what you really keep. Don't go below is the lowest sticker that still hits your target. Break-even is where you stop making anything at all. Safe discount is how far you can come down at a show.

Small cards lose money fast. On a $5 card, a 13% fee plus a dollar of postage takes about 92% of the gross margin. Cards marked no room can't be discounted at all — they're already at or under break-even as priced.

04Selling a card

Easier on a computer than a phone. Same login, same shop, same inventory.

1

Set the cost first

What you paid for it. Scanned cards come in at zero.

Why: profit is worked out from cost. Leave it at zero and every sale looks like pure profit.
2

Change status to Sold

Enter the real sale price and the fee you were charged.

Why: gross and net profit are calculated for you and can't be edited afterwards.
3

Check the Dashboard

Realized Profit only counts cards sold through this form. Everything else on that screen is an estimate.

Why: it's the difference between money you made and money you might make.
Check the sale price before you save. A recorded sale can be corrected afterwards, but profit figures feed straight into your Dashboard — a wrong number there is a wrong number everywhere until you fix it.

05When something looks wrong

Every one of these has a cause worth knowing rather than a button to press.

It found fewer cards than I put down

Almost always spacing or glare. Spread the cards further apart, angle shiny cards away from the light, and shoot from further back.

Shiny and refractor cards are the hardest — a bright reflection can hide an edge entirely. If one card keeps getting missed, move that one and re-shoot.

The year or card number says Unknown

You skipped backs, or the back photo didn't come out. The year and number are printed on the back and can't reliably be read from the front.

The price you get is then for that player generally, not that card — which can be off by a lot. Re-scan with backs.

A common card is showing a huge price

The card was identified wrongly and the search matched something else — a sealed box or a lot. You'll see it flagged.

Don't add it. Re-scan that card on its own with the back captured. If it comes back sensible, the first read was the problem.

A price looks far too low

Cheap cards often sell at auction for almost nothing while fixed-price copies go for several times more. If the card is flagged as limited or thin, there weren't enough clean sales to price from.

Check the sold comps link on the tile and judge it yourself.

I added the same cards twice

Add all to inventory ran twice. Delete the duplicates from the Inventory tab — they'll have the same timestamp.

Press once and wait for the green confirmation rather than pressing again.

My staff can't see the inventory

They need to be invited to the shop and signed in on their own account. Same shop, same cards, any device.

A blank Inventory tab usually means signed out, not missing data. Sign in and it comes back.

06Running a break

Break Manager holds the spots; the Dashboard tells you whether the break made money.

1

Create the break

Name it, choose how many spots, pick team or random.

2

Fill in each spot

Customer, team, and what that spot sold for.

Why: spot prices are where break revenue comes from. Leave them empty and the break reads as zero regardless of what you took.
3

Enter your costs

Wax cost, shipping, platform fee.

Why: profit is revenue minus those three. Without them you're looking at revenue, not profit.
4

Assign cards as you pull

Auto-assign matches scanned cards to spots by team; anything unmatched you place by hand.
